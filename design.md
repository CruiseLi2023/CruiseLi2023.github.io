Unit 4 - Day 24 - Assignment: Design Process Summary in Innovator Journal
1. Interview
Who I talked to: My brother.

What we discussed & learned: We discussed what it feels like to transition onto a new campus and keep track of daily responsibilities during the first few weeks. I learned that arriving at a new school comes with a massive amount of information to remember all at once—from finding where buildings and classrooms are located to keeping up with daily schedules, sports, meetings, and school routines without feeling lost or overwhelmed.

2. Principles
Problem Space: Helping a new student quickly learn their way around campus and stay on top of their daily schedule, activities, and school routines with confidence.
What was important to this person: Independence, centralized information, and simplicity. They needed a reliable, easy-to-use tool that fits naturally into everyday routines without requiring constant help from others.

3. Raw Ideas
Campus Map: Create a map showing all the buildings, classrooms, offices, and important locations.   
Daily Campus Walk: Walk around campus every day to become familiar with the buildings and locations.   
Digital Calendar: Use a calendar that shows class times, activities, deadlines, and reminders.   
Personal Advisor: Assign a student advisor who helps him understand the campus, schedule, and school routines.   
Campus Scavenger Hunt: Create a game where he has to find different classrooms and important locations around campus.   
AI Personal Assistant: Have an AI assistant that tells him where to go, what to do next, and reminds him of upcoming activities.   
GPS Bracelet: Wear a bracelet that vibrates and gives directions whenever he needs to go somewhere.   
Student Tour Guide: Have an experienced student accompany him for his first few weeks and show him around.   
Holographic Campus Guide: Create a hologram that shows a 3D model of campus and lets him see the fastest routes to different locations.   
Campus Navigation Robot: Have a personal robot follow him around and guide him to every class, activity, and meeting.   

4. 3 Best Ideas

Digital Calendar
Create an online calendar that includes everything the student needs to do throughout the week.   
Include all classes, activities, sports, meetings, deadlines, and school events.   
Each event would show the exact time and location.   
Add reminders before classes and activities so the student knows when they need to leave and where they need to go.   
Allow the student to view their daily and weekly schedules in one place.   

Campus Map
Create a digital or printed map showing all the important buildings, classrooms, offices, and other locations on campus.   
Label each building so the student can easily recognize different places.   
Include walking routes between buildings to help students find the fastest way to their next class.   
Make the map easy to access on a phone so students can check it whenever they need help.   

Student Tour Guide
Pair a new student with an experienced student who already knows the campus.   
Have the experienced student show them important locations, classrooms, dining areas, and other facilities.   
The guide could explain school routines and answer questions about daily life.   
The student guide could accompany the new student during their first few weeks.   
This would help new students feel more comfortable and become familiar with the campus.   


5. Experiment & Path Forward
I chose to try the Digital Calendar idea because I thought it would be the most practical and easiest to use every day. I created an online calendar to organize everything the student needs to know about their schedule. It includes classes, activities, sports, meetings, deadlines, and school events. Each event has its exact time and location, along with reminders so the student knows when and where they need to go.   
The calendar also allows the student to look at their entire week ahead of time instead of only focusing on the current day. This makes it easier to prepare for upcoming activities, manage time, and avoid forgetting important events. I think this idea is helpful because new students have a lot of information to remember when they first arrive at a school. Instead of checking different places for their schedule, they can find everything in one online calendar. It also gives them more independence because they can check where they need to go without constantly asking someone else. 
Overall, I think this is a useful solution because it is simple, easy to update, and can become part of the student's everyday routine. We will revisit this after Thanksgiving break!   

6. Reflections
What I learned from the experience: I learned that designing for a real person requires understanding their actual daily frustrations rather than just guessing what might help them. Talking directly to my brother revealed how stressful small logistical details can be when everything else is new.
What was most interesting, useful, or surprising: It was interesting to see how brainstorming extreme ideas like navigation robots or holographic campus guides ultimately helped clarify why a simple, everyday tool like a digital calendar is the most practical solution.
What I will take with you from this experience: I will take away the importance of empathy interviews and user-centered thinking to make sure the solutions I build actually solve real-world problems.
